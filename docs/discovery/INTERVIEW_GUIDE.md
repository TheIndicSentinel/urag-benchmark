# Interview guide (20-30 min)

Open: "This is research; I have nothing to sell. Is it OK if I take notes? I'll record your role, not your name. Please skip anything confidential."

## 1. The incident (8-10 min)
- Think of a recent incident where investigation was slow or went wrong. What happened?
- What was the first signal? Who picked it up?
- What did you need to know, and where did you look? In what order?
- What did you find; what did you not find?

## 2. Evidence problems (6-8 min)
- Was any information stale, contradictory, missing, or something you couldn't access?
- Did you ever follow a runbook or doc that turned out to be outdated or for a different service/environment?
- How did you decide which doc to trust? Who owns runbooks, and how do they get updated?
- Did a recent change come up as a suspect? How did you decide whether it was actually the cause?

## 3. Current tools (4-5 min)
- What do you use today (ITSM, search, wiki, chat, AI features)? What does it do well, and where does it fall short?
- Have you tried an AI assistant on this? What went wrong or right?

## 4. Impact and value (3-4 min)
- How long did this take; who was pulled in; what was the consequence?
- How often does something like this happen?
- What would have to improve for you to change your workflow? What would make you reject a new tool?

## 5. Access and decision (3 min)
- Who owns this problem, and who decides on and funds tooling? What security review would a new tool need?
- Could you share a sanitized example later? Would your team test something in a bounded way?

Close: "Who else should I talk to?"

## Do not
Lead with solutions, ask "would you use X", describe the product, or let positive reactions stand in for commitment.
